# Whitsundays starter collection: editorial review draft

Prepared 28 September 2026, Australia/Brisbane. **Draft only; no catalogue publication, Supabase seed, reviewed route, or navigational approval.**

The requested starter collection covers Airlie, Daydream, Hamilton/Hamo, Nara, Tongue and Butterfly. The structured research manifest is `services/dayPlanner/whitsundaysStarter.ts`. It contains six destination candidates and five Airlie-to-destination trip ideas. These are editorial priorities, not promises that each trip fits a day, the vessel, the weather or the return window.

## What this draft can support

The manifest can support an editor choosing exact endpoints, reviewing sources and preparing original destination copy. It has local keys and aliases, source URLs and retrieval dates, draft summaries, activity research leads and unresolved decisions. All positions are `null`, review credentials are absent and route variants are empty. No sailed or private tracks were consulted or copied. It has no runtime imports, network calls or database writes.

This format intentionally differs from `CatalogueDetail` and the database row format. The Supabase catalogue requires a coordinate pair and complete evidence/licensing metadata even for a pending version; these research leads do not yet meet those requirements. Do not substitute `0,0`, an island centre, the first search result, a geocoder result or an old anchorage coordinate to make an insert pass. A place name is insufficient evidence for a boat endpoint.

The existing local Whitsundays pilot contains Nara and Tongue, among four other destinations. Their existing keys are cross-references only: neither their old review date nor their mapped reference positions automatically become review evidence for the new global catalogue. This draft does not replace that pilot.

### Dated notices that must survive editorial review

- **Nara / southern Hook Island, 6–15 October 2026 inclusive:** the [official alert](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts/26934) announces a control operation, a 200 m seaward exclusion zone around southern Hook Island and Ngaro Cultural Site closure from 08:00 to 15:00 each day. The linked maritime notice must establish the exact exclusion extent before any route content is reviewed. Do not infer that entry elsewhere in the inlet or outside those hours is approved. The existing local planner omits Nara for the whole date; that conservative app rule is distinct from the notice's daily shore-closure hours.
- **Tongue Point, 12–16 October 2026:** [the official alert](https://parks.qld.gov.au/park-alerts/26977) describes temporary closure of some facilities, with at least one lookout remaining open. Toilets close on 14–15 October and for shorter maintenance periods. This is not evidence that the whole bay or all walks close. Works are weather dependent.

These dates use `Australia/Brisbane`. The global catalogue's current schema has no structured closure dates or timezone field; free-text limitations cannot reliably enforce date exclusions. Publishing a candidate with a material date restriction needs either an explicit supported access-policy representation or a publication decision that withholds the affected candidate. Do not silently drop the existing Nara exclusion during migration.

### Source conflict caught during research

An older [Daydream document on the operator's former CDN](https://document-tc.galaxy.tf/wdpdf-8ng2lhzhuk5hbk40n6eqbclxu/file.pdf) appears in search results and says passengers need a visitor pass/accommodation booking and must not sleep aboard. The current marina page links the July 2026 document, which allows suitably equipped vessels to stay overnight. The old document is **not** used as current access evidence in the manifest. Confirm current visitor inclusions with the operator before publication; do not copy its older pass requirement or overnight prohibition into the new catalogue.

## Trip ideas and promotion requirements

The five ideas are Airlie–Daydream, Airlie–Hamilton, Airlie–Nara, Airlie–Tongue and Airlie–Butterfly. Each needs a specific departure and destination version, separate outbound and return review, and any required intermediate controls. Neither straight lines between place names nor reversal of outbound controls establish a return route. No time, distance, depth, clearance or shelter claim is supplied.

Before a future publication decision, an editor must resolve:

1. The exact destination identity, boat endpoint and source provenance, keeping shore activities separate from navigation controls.
2. A licence/permission and attribution record for each material used, including the intended use of any spatial data.
3. Current access notices and a way to enforce any date restrictions; operator confirmation is separate from public-source review.
4. Original bounded copy, appropriate activity tags, limitations and a real reviewer/scope/review date/expiry. `lunch` in the existing local pilot means a picnic; restaurant availability must not be smuggled into that tag.
5. Version-pinned trip endpoints and separately reviewed directional route variants if routes are to be published, with evidence for every required checkpoint. A past private sailed track is neither available for this collection nor automatic navigational evidence.
6. Validation of the eventual content, access-policy behaviour and intended client presentation. Source research, automated data checks and on-water validation must be reported separately.

This draft is complete as an editorial preparation artifact. Publication and live seeding remain outside the current work.
