# Plan Your Day: today on the water (build 124)

Shane, 2026-10-08: "ok, can you revamp the plan your day thing, it does nothing of use at the moment claude. make it work please ;)", then "you have carte blanche on your Plan your day!".

The old planner routed every stop through the inshore router before it showed anything. From Coral Sea Marina its 3-hour default ruled out all six Whitsundays stops, and with more hours it still refused every route out, because the marina approach is charted 1.8 m against Serene Summer's 2.4 m draft. It also sat behind the Auto route (trial) switch, and its answers went stale after 15 minutes. It was replaced in build 124.

Plan Your Day now opens straight onto today wherever the boat is. There is no form. It never routes, never saves, never expires and never blocks: it gives cautions, not blocks, as Cast Off does.

## What the skipper sees

**Screen 1: Today.** A centred card, clear of the tab bar, showing:

- **The day.** Morning, afternoon and evening, each **Inside**, **Near** or **Over your wind limits** (or **No forecast**, or **Past** for a part of today already gone). They are rated on the wind and gusts at the place: the sea is read only along the way to each stop, so a part never claims it, and Sources says so. Each word carries a glyph, so colour is never the only signal. Thunder in 2 or more models shows in the cell of the part it falls in ("Thunder", held at Near, with a drawn bolt for its ≈). The limits are the skipper's own (below).
- **Day chips.** Today plus two more days, each with that day's best part in her limits' glyph and colour ("✓ Fri", "≈ Today"; build 127, 127-PYD-1: ✓ ≈ ✕ ? always mean her limits, and the models' agreement glyphs stay on The Glass). VoiceOver hears the level and whether the models agree.
- **Stay.** 1 h, 2 h (the default) or 4 h ashore, or Overnight (until 09:00 local the next day).
- **One headline**, chosen in this order: "Stay put…" when all three parts are over her limits (with a link to a lighter day if one of the chips has one), "Models split…" when the best part is split, "Inside your wind limits all day…", "{Morning}'s your window: inside your wind limits until about…", or "Near your limits at best…". Thunder that holds the part after the window at Near takes the place of "… gets near your limits." ("Morning's your window: inside your wind limits until about 12:00, then thunder in 3 of 7 models.", whether the thunder or the wind closed the window: thunder is never inside it), and a Near day adds "Thunder from about 13:00 in 3 of 7 models." once (without "about" where it would run long): never a second clause, and no headline grows past the window's 98 characters (appended, it ran to five lines at 320 px; "… 12:00. Thunder from about 13:00 …" made 108 and a fourth line at 390 px). Some spread among the models adds "Some spread." where it keeps the headline within the window's 98 characters. "Too late for a day out… Showing tomorrow." opens on tomorrow when less than the stay plus 2 h is left before last light. With no forecast it says "No forecast for today: places shown, weather not checked."
- **Facts.** First and last light, and the next high and low water in daylight: "☀ 05:31–18:36 · HW 10:52 · LW 17:03". Where no tide station answers it says "No tide prediction here".
- **One notice**, when one applies, by priority: a tropical cyclone within 600 NM, a boat position over 6 h old, the default boat ("Typical 6 kn boat: set yours in Vessel ›"), offline, a forecast that failed, or OpenStreetMap data more than a day old ("Map data from 12 Sep"). The rest go to Sources.
- **The best stops.** Two rows below 640 px tall, three above. Each row has a glyph (✓ inside, ≈ near, ✕ over, ? unknown), the name, a shelter word and an amber "Local notes" tag for reviewed stops (a reviewed note is often a caution; the note itself names its reviewer), then the times, short so they can be big (126-17c): "07:30 → 09:40 · back 15:10" (overnight: "07:30 → 09:40 · about 14 NM"), which VoiceOver reads in words, "Leave 07:30, arrive 09:40, back home 15:10" (overnight: "… about 14 nautical miles"). Times come only from a route forecast that loaded; otherwise "About 14 NM · weather not checked". A ✕ or ? row shows its reason where its times were (127-PYD-1): "SE 30 kn on the way", "Home after dark (18:28)", "⚡ Thunder on the way home", "Chop for the stay (SE 21 kn)", "Swell for the stay (1.8 m SSE)", "Weather didn't load"; a long reason has a short row form (at most 30 characters, so the row keeps its two lines), and VoiceOver, Not today and the stop page say it whole ("over your limits: open to the SE — SE 21 kn has 15+ NM of fetch", once). Its times are on the stop page. In Queensland the offline atlas's stops show at once while OpenStreetMap is still answering; with nothing in yet the row says "Finding places…", and when no place source answered, "Places didn't load: OpenStreetMap didn't answer." (All places waits for places in either case).
- **Footer.** All places (N), the official warnings issuer for the place (Bureau of Meteorology, MeteoAlarm, NWS…), and the credit line naming the models that answered, ending "Not a clearance".

**A stop's detail.** It opens on its verdict when the stop is not Inside ("✕ Over your limits: thunder in 3 of 7 models on the way home", "≈ Near your limits: SE 15 kn on the way", "≈ Near your limits: SE 18 kn works across 8.7 NM — expect chop"), in its level's colour; a row below that would say the same (the stay's reason, "Home after dark (18:28)") leaves it to the verdict. Then how each time was worked out: leave and there (with how she sails it and the wind), ashore or at anchor with the shelter reason, a reviewed stop's own Parks notes one per line under the stay, then the landing window where Queensland Parks name a landing tide (the note says the tide, the window row gives the times, so the tide is said once) (Cid Harbour's shark warning, Chance Bay's south-easterlies; the catalogue's shared boilerplate is said once, as "Not a clearance"), home, the light, the sea on the way, the distance and how it was measured, and "Leaving a marina: check its approach depth against the tide before you go." when she starts in one. Leave chips are the best departure's window; a tap recomputes in place. **Plot on chart** sets the Plan page's departure and opens the Manual plotter with straight pins. It fits outright at ordinary text from 375×667; at 320×568 a reviewed stop's Parks notes push the later rows into a scroll (the first note is in view as it opens), because the notes are never dropped to make it fit.

**All places** has three lists. **Fits** holds only stops whose own route weather was checked and is not over her limits (never on a "Stay put" day). **Weather not checked** holds the rest of the ranked stops that the wind at the place leaves room for (beyond the best three, offline, still loading, or past the end of the forecast). **Not today** gives every other place a plain reason: "closed 6–15 Oct (Queensland Parks)", "home after dark (18:36) at your speed", "home in the last of the light", "over your limits: …" (on the way, or "SE 29 kn in the area" when every hourly departure meets an hour over her limits at the place), "over your limits most of the day (a gap: leave 07:00)", "no anchoring here", "about 26 NM each way — too far for 2 h ashore", "weather didn't load", or "not ranked — 40 closer places checked first".

**Sources and limits** names whose limits these are and lets her change them in place: an embedded ComfortQuickConfig writing her Comfort settings, its wind, gust and sea sliders starting from the limits in force ("Sliders you haven't moved show your boat's limits"), never the editor's own 35 kt / 4 m, and without the angle bands only the router reads. It says the day is rated on wind and gusts, and the sea only along the way. It also lists which models answered and which did not, every credit, the forecast time, places that did not load, what is not checked, and the official warnings link.

**Plan from** offers her boat (bus, Pi or cloud, with its age; signed out it says to sign in), this phone, the home port and saved places, or a typed place or "lat, lon". With no position the sheet asks before it shows anything else.

## Where the numbers come from

- **The place.** The start's IANA zone is resolved offline from its coordinates (`resolveTimeZone`), never the phone's. Every time, part of the day and date is on the place's clock. Light is civil first and last light worked out on the phone. Under the midnight sun the day is planned 06:00–20:00 local (14 h) and says "Light all day: plan capped at 14 h". Where the sun never rises it says "No daylight here today" and lists no stops.
- **Her limits.** Her Comfort settings first: the value she set is "over", and 80 % of it is "near". Otherwise `vesselWindowThresholds` for her boat, with wave limits converted from feet to metres. Otherwise a typical cruiser. Sources says which applied. The old fixed 20 kn / 25 kn / 1.5 m caps are gone.
- **The day.** One seven-model point forecast (`queryModelSpread`: 2 proxy calls, memoised per 0.1° cell for 30 min and shared with the Glass; `weather_code` was added for thunder). Each hour takes the median wind, the circular-mean direction and the highest gust. A part is Over if any hourly median reaches her wind limit or any gust reaches her gust limit. It is Near if the median reaches 80 %, any one model reaches her limit, 2 or more models show thunder, or the models are split. Fewer than 2 models in an hour is No forecast. **A missing gust never passes:** with no gust from any model the part is capped at Near.
- **The places, worldwide first.** OpenStreetMap anchorages from at most four one-degree reference cells; only the cells leave the phone. Cells over a day old are used and dated, not refused. Queensland adds the offline anchorage atlas, with its baked land fetch and GBRMPA no-anchoring areas; the atlas wins the dedupe. Inside a reviewed region the Whitsundays stops are joined on by anchorage id ([day-planner-destinations.md](day-planner-destinations.md)). Marinas only name the start and give the marina line; they are never destinations. A point that reports a restriction goes to "no anchoring here".
- **Reach.** Up to 30 NM, never under 3 NM, from her cruising speed, the light left and the stay. Points under 1 NM from the start are dropped. The 40 nearest are ranked; the reviewed stops are always among them, and the same name within 2.5 NM counts as one place.
- **Shelter is land only.** Atlas points use their land fetch table. A reviewed stop whose own Parks note names a wind (Chance Bay's south-easterlies, Maureen's Cove's northerlies: `accessWinds`) is held at "Some chop" in 10 kn or more from there, whatever the land table says. OpenStreetMap points get one ray-cast against the OSM coastline (km to NM, capped at 15). With no coastline the stop reads "Shelter not known". Reefs are never counted as shelter. Stay comfort uses `scoreAnchorage` (graded, copes with unknown swell), with its hour on the place's clock. Grades show as Very sheltered, Sheltered, Some chop and Exposed.
- **Distance is said honestly.** A saved route of hers that starts within 0.5 NM of the start and ends within 0.5 NM of the stop (either way round) gives its own length and shape. Otherwise it is the straight line × 1.15 when clear of the coastline, × 1.4 when it crosses it, or × 1.3 when the coastline is unknown, always shown as "about".
- **The pre-rank** scores every candidate cheaply in the area wind: shelter grade, reviewed stops first within a grade, then score, then distance. Only the top three get their own forecasts along the route.
- **Route forecasts**, for those three only. There is one route spread (the passage HUD's five models, so the two share a cache) and one sea request (MFWAM, with the snap guard: a station snapped too far is "no reading", never a number) along the **one-way** leg. The trip home is that leg turned round (`mirror.ts`). A second request is never made, because `routeStations` spaces stations at least 10 NM apart: a round trip under 20 NM would sample the start twice and never the stop. The chart's own wind model heads it; if that model did not answer, the first that did is used and named.
- **Departures.** Hourly from the earliest leave (now + 30 min today, first light on other days), at most 14. The Plan page's departure is never a floor: it may be left from another passage or an earlier Plot on chart, and it used to hide the morning and declare "too late" at breakfast. Each leg is walked with `planPassage` in the headline wind with her polar (or a typical cruising polar), the way the passage HUD does, and stretched by the distance factor when the distance is an estimate. A departure is Over for wind, gust or sea on the way, an hour over her limits at the place (the day's own strip) while she is under way, thunder in 2+ models underway, home after dark, or a poor stay. It is Near for 80 % of a limit, any one model over, a split, home in the last 30 min of light, a tenable stay, no gust from any model, no wave reading anywhere on the way (her sea limit unchecked), or shelter not known. The end of the forecast, or a speed only assumed, is Unknown, never Inside. The best departure and its ≤3 h window are ranked by `departureScore` and `compactWindow`, extracted from `suggestPassageDeparture` so the HUD and the planner agree.
- **Tides.** One WorldTides call when the sheet opens (Pi cache first, cached 24 h). The landing window at a reviewed stop whose Parks note says mid to high tide (Tongue Bay, Chance Bay, Cid Harbour, Maureen's Cove) is asked only when its detail opens, and is labelled approximate.
- **Cost.** A cold open is about 8 Open-Meteo calls through the proxy (2 for the point block, then 2 for each of three stops), one WorldTides credit at most, up to four reference cells and one coastline query, and one cyclone call. Changing the day or the stay is a pure recompute. Results never expire; reopening after the 30–60 min caches refetches, and Sources shows the forecast time.
- **Fences.** Every wait has a wall-clock deadline (30 s for each source of the place, 45 s for a stop's legs) and stops dead when the sheet closes or the account changes. A source that fails says so: a failed forecast reads "Forecast unavailable right now", never offline and never a good day; places that could not be read are unknown, never "no anchorages mapped".

## Never claimed

That a place is safe, legal or holds. Depth or tide over the route, or the marina approach: the planner reads no depth, which is why it no longer asks to confirm the draft. The draft is still confirmed where routing or depth grading happens. Also never claimed: currents in the times, reefs as shelter, gusts funnelling round islands, or "no storms" (thunder comes only from the models' weather codes). Distances are "about" unless they come from her saved route. "Not a clearance" appears in the credit line, the detail footnote and Sources, and nowhere else. Attribution names the models that actually answered, which is a licence condition.

## Plot on chart

`setPlanDeparture` (`services/planDeparture.ts`, extracted from DepartControl with the same key, account-scoped storage and event; DepartControl now listens for it) sets the departure. A `plot-day` tracer request then loads the pins into the **Manual** plotter as an unsaved draft, fitted on the chart: start → stop → start for a day out, start → stop overnight, or her saved route (an Auto route runs to hundreds of points: the chart takes up to 19,999 pins, a 10,000-point saved route there and back; anything longer is sent as straight pins, and pins the chart cannot use get a line on the chart rather than a silent empty plotter). A line explains that the pins are straight and that the Route report checks them against her charts. Both ⚡ buttons in the chart's plotter stay parked; an Auto handoff is a build 125 item. The request is identity-fenced, because it carries the boat's position.

## Gates

Pro only (owner decision 2026-10-01; every account is Pro during the public beta). Sign-in and installed charts are no longer needed: the planner does not route. The Auto route (trial) switch gates the Auto workspace only ([AUTOROUTING_TRIAL.md](AUTOROUTING_TRIAL.md)). The sheet is lazy, and the entry tile imports nothing from `services/dayPlanner`.

## Known limits

- The leave window ignores the marina's own tide gate. At Coral Sea Marina "leave 07:00" can come before the approach clears for 2.4 m. The detail says so; build 125 is to remember a berth's tide gate from the route report.
- Straight-line estimates can under-read routes round Whitsunday Island. The ×1.4 crossing factor and the "about" soften this, but a stop near the light limit may not fit once the route is drawn.
- Area wind pre-ranks every stop. Funnelling in the Whitsunday Passage is seen only for the top three.
- Inshore, MFWAM snapping means many anchorages honestly read "no reading".
- The shared cruising-trip catalogue ([CRUISING_TRIP_CATALOGUE.md](CRUISING_TRIP_CATALOGUE.md)) is parked out of the UI. Its client, migration and CI check stay; it needs reviewed content and a router that can chain checkpoints.

Later (125+): the Auto handoff, a berth's tide gate in the leave window, real water distances on the inshore grid in a Worker, a 4 h rain line, QPWS and OSM moorings as stops, one scorer shared with Anchorage Tonight, thunder along the route, picking the start on the map, and a separate licence check of the GBRMPA spatial terms (not a blocker: the atlas already ships in the map's anchorage layer).

Reviewed destination sources and closure records are snapshots kept by a person. `verifiedAt` is when the activity facts were reviewed, not a live access check, and nothing refreshes park alerts or closures in the background. Closure dates use the destination's own zone (Australia/Brisbane for the Whitsundays), and are checked against the planned local date before anything is scored.
